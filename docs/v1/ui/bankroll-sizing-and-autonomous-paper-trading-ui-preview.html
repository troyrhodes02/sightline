Design handoff · v1.0

A ledger that can testify against itself.

Seven admin-only surfaces through which a bot trades fake money and then accounts for every dollar of it: the stake it wanted, the stake that filled, and the constraint that explains the difference. What deliberately does not ship is any control that moves the system from paper to real money — there is no such affordance in any state, including when readiness passes. The commitment to check these frames against: intended and filled are two numbers, always both, and every candidate carries a named binding constraint. Use the Light / Dark switch to flip every product surface.

Note This preview is a rendering of the design, not product code. The application is built in Material UI against the theme; nothing here is copied into it.

What these screens are built to respect

The design commitments a reviewer should check the frames above against.

Honest accounting

  • Intended and filled are two numbers, always both. Every candidate, every position row, and every review summary carries the intended stake beside the simulated fill, with the shortfall named.
  • Every candidate names its binding constraint. The bound-by field is a closed set of eleven values and is never blank — including "none" when nothing bound it.
  • Fills are top-of-book only. The displayed size is shown on every priced candidate, and a fill capped by it says so rather than walking deeper into the book.
  • Nothing happened is a result. A no-candidate cycle, a skipped cutoff, an offseason, and an unenabled bot each render as a finished statement with its reason.
  • Money is neutral-toned. Ledger dollars are Sightline's own fiction and never borrow the market's mint; only a sign takes colour, and the sign is always printed.

Safety and separation

  • Kill, Resume, and Force Override are never adjacent. Kill has no confirmation and is always reachable; Force Override is a separate route with per-condition acknowledgement and all four facts stated before the button enables.
  • An override never ages into "cleared". It stays a warning-toned permanent record in Review, with its actor and time.
  • Eligibility is a report, not a button. No control in any state moves the system from paper to real money, and the disclaimer is strongest when every criterion passes.
  • The replay writes nowhere the bankroll can read it. It sits below a hard rule, states its own boundary, and never contributes to a figure above it.
  • Paper says paper, permanently. Every surface carries the simulated disclosure in its own chrome, and no shared viewer surface hints that any of this exists.
