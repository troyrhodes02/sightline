A container that is finished even though the product inside it is not.

Pitch 3 ships the invite-only shell, the Material UI theme, and the access model — and no product data at all. The screens below are the whole surface: two entry flows, a role-aware shell, one placeholder, health with honest not-yet-available states, access management, and every terminal state. The commitment to check them against is that a viewer can never see, or infer, that the admin's private layer exists.

Note This preview is a rendering of the design, not product code. The application is built in Material UI against the theme; nothing here is copied into it. Superseded in part The access model changed from invite-only to request-and-approve on 2026-08-01. The five invitation frames in section 01 no longer exist; sign-up and an approve/deny queue replace them. Every other frame — the shell, slate placeholder, health, settings, terminal states, tokens and type — is current.

What these screens are built to respect

The design commitments to check the frames above against.

Access and privacy

  • Absence is indistinguishable from non-existence. A viewer's shell has two items. Health and Users are not greyed, blurred, or locked — they are not there.
  • The interface never renders ahead of the server. A viewer at /users gets the denial at that URL, with no admin chrome drawn first and no client-side redirect.
  • One message for both credential failures. An unknown email and a wrong password read identically; a provider outage reads differently, because someone who typed the right password should not be told otherwise.
  • No signup, no recovery link, no credential field. Nothing anywhere implies a viewer could trade through Sightline or hand over a Kalshi key.

Honesty about what does not exist

  • A shell that admits it is a shell. The slate placeholder has no mock contracts, no sample rows, no disabled filters, and no skeleton — it has nothing to fetch.
  • Four kinds of unavailable, not one. Not yet implemented, never run, not expected, and failed are distinct. No job carries a fabricated timestamp; a healthy job carries no badge at all.
  • Destructive copy names its subject. “Revoke access for dana@example.com?” — never “Are you sure?”. The row is removed after the write succeeds, never optimistically.
  • Appearance lives in Settings. Not in the app bar, not in the account menu, not behind a floating sun. System is the default and applies before first paint.
