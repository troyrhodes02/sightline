A ranked disagreement list that admits what it does not know, and remembers what he decided.

This pitch ships the rolling pre-kickoff slate, the contract detail view with drivers and distribution, and the admin's take/fade/skip capture. It deliberately does not ship staleness marking, settlement, accuracy, or anything that places an order. The commitment to check every frame against: uncertainty travels with its number, and viewers see no trace of decisions.

Note This preview is a rendering of the design, not product code. The application is built in Material UI against the theme; nothing here is copied into it.

What these screens are built to respect

The design commitments a reviewer should check the frames above against.

Honest numbers

  • Uncertainty travels with the number. Every probability carries confidence; every price carries observed-at; every projection carries computed-at and cutoff — in the list, not only on detail.
  • Provenance by palette. Indigo is the model, mint is the market, amber is caution, rose is negative edge. Edge also carries sign and glyph, so the encoding survives greyscale.
  • Nothing is fabricated. A missing edge is an em-dash. No projection, no price, unresolved, degraded, and empty are designed states — never invented numbers, never an error page.
  • The ask drives ranking. The executable price is the primary market number; the midpoint is labelled context on detail.

Private decisions, equal rows

  • Viewers see no trace of decisions. No chip, no blank column, no disabled control, no payload field. Absence is indistinguishable from non-existence.
  • Took, faded, skipped are three states. Unmarked is the absence of a row and renders as nothing. No disposition is ever forced, and a decision needs no recommendation.
  • Row height never changes. Recommended, de-emphasised, no-projection, and disposition-marked rows share one shape at every breakpoint, so columns scan.
  • Unresolved is retained, not hidden. Both roles see the contract; only the admin sees diagnostics and the one-step mapping correction.
